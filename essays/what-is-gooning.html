What is Gooning?

November 20, 2026

I feel like such a goon right now. And I must clarify what goon actually means, before I use it in the infinitely flexible, insurmountably vague ways that I bend and stretch and gesticulate with this word.

In my vocabulary, “goon” or “gooning” means to do something in verb form. It is a synonym of do, but in a sillier way. To “goon” with Claude, for example, means that you worked with Claude, in a mildly serious way. To “goon” all the time, it means that you did something, you existed, you surely did some activity, but you are not too sure what. It was not bad, but it was not good, and it is a bit strange. Just existing. Scrolling for 10 minutes here, replying to text messages here, going down to get a glass of water and coming back, then your friend asks, “What did you do?” That is a prime example of “Oh, I was just gooning.”

A goon, or “goon behavior,” is in noun form. And in a noun form, it describes someone. You are starting to see why this word is so cherished in my dictionary. A goon can be your friend that is silly, your buddy, an annoying person you know at school, or frankly anyone.

But it usually has a slightly negative, lazy, unproductive connotation. The slightest bit, like six-hour-old tap water. It is not bad water, but you know there is a slight hint of something strange. To feel like a goon is not a good thing. Gooning all the time is not a good thing. Certainly, it is not making you go up with goodness.
